# 01 · Write it down

**Wall:** the record. **Time:** about 7 minutes.

**Objective.** The learner can run a brief: let the agent ask, answer in a paragraph, accept
or change its recommended answers, and recognise that the output is a numbered list of
decisions the builder will work from.

## Screen 1 · Where did the timer come from?

> The timer came from a gap. So did the fifth shelf, the leaderboard and "the standard
> fridge rules". The agent did not know what the client's course needs, and you did not tell
> it, so it guessed. The fix is not a longer prompt. It is a conversation where the agent asks
> and you answer, until nothing is left that would change what gets built.

Aside, **brief**: *the conversation before anything is built, where the agent asks you ranked
questions a few at a time and the decisions come out numbered.*

## Screen 2 · How a brief goes

Five short cards, the workflow's own rules:

1. **You describe it freely first.** A paragraph in your own words, however rough.
2. **The agent reads before it asks.** What exists already, the client's materials, the
   standard if there is one. Then it asks.
3. **At most five questions a round, ranked.** The question whose answer settles the most
   others comes first. Each comes with what it decides and the agent's recommended answer, so
   "your defaults, except number 3" is a complete reply.
4. **You answer in a paragraph.** The agent plays back what it took each answer to be,
   re-asks only what you missed, and asks the next round.
5. **It stops when the rest is a builder's call.** Then you get the decisions: D1, D2, D3…,
   one sentence each with its reason, on one record the builder reads.

Quote card, Ross: *You may not know what you want until you see the first round. The brief is
where you find out what you do know.*

## Screen 3 · Try it: the fridge brief

The fake agent, after a fixed opening from the learner:

> **You:** A fridge activity for a client's food-hygiene course. Learners drag food onto the
> right shelf and get told why when they're wrong. It's for kitchen staff. The authoring
> tool can't do it.

> **Agent:** I read the client's food-hygiene guide and the course outline. Five questions,
> ranked. My recommended answer is under each; say "your defaults" or pick differently.
>
> 1. **Who does this, and where?** Decides the device and how long it can take.
>    *Recommended: kitchen staff on induction, on a shared tablet or a laptop, about five
>    minutes.*
> 2. **Which shelves, which items, and whose rules?** Decides the whole content.
>    *Recommended: four shelves (top, middle, bottom, salad drawer) and eight items, with the
>    rules taken from the client's guide, not from me.*
> 3. **What happens on a wrong drop?** Decides the feedback and whether it is a test or a
>    practice. *Recommended: the item goes back, a one-line reason appears, they try again;
>    no limit on tries.*
> 4. **How does it end, and is there a score?** Decides what the LMS records.
>    *Recommended: it ends when all eight are placed; the score is how many were right first
>    time, out of eight; wrong ones are listed with their reason.*
> 5. **Keyboard and touch?** Decides accessibility and the tablet.
>    *Recommended: yes to both. Enter picks an item up, arrows move between shelves, Enter
>    places it; a screen reader hears the item, the shelf and the result. Drag works on
>    touch.*

The learner answers each question by picking one of three options. Option one is always
"the recommended answer"; the others are plausible alternatives (for Q3, for example: "three
tries then it's placed for them" and "no feedback until the end, it's a test"). Every option
is accepted; the agent plays back what it took and the decision fills in on the right as
D1–D5, worded for the option chosen. Nothing here is a wrong turn; a brief has no wrong
answers, only unanswered ones.

Then round two:

> **Agent:** Took your answers as above (D1–D5 on the right). Three more; the rest I can
> leave to the builder.
>
> 6. **A timer?** *Recommended: no. Induction, not a game; a timer punishes the people the
>    course is for.*
> 7. **Can they retry the whole thing, and which attempt does the LMS get?**
>    *Recommended: a Try again button; the LMS gets the latest attempt.*
> 8. **What does the client see afterwards?** *Recommended: completion and the score per
>    learner, nothing else.*

The learner answers; D6–D8 fill in. The agent closes:

> **Agent:** That's the brief. Everything left (how the shelves are drawn, what the drag feels
> like, the exact wording) is the builder's call and does not change what the learner sees
> you decide. Eight decisions on the record.

The decisions panel now shows D1–D8. If the learner took every recommendation they read:

- D1 Kitchen staff on induction, on a tablet or laptop, about five minutes.
- D2 Four shelves and eight items: top: cheese, yoghurt; middle: cooked chicken, leftover
  lasagne (sealed); bottom: raw chicken, raw salmon; salad drawer: lettuce, tomatoes. Rules
  and reasons from the client's guide.
- D3 A wrong drop returns the item and shows a one-line reason; unlimited tries.
- D4 Ends when all eight are placed; score is right-first-time out of eight; wrong ones
  listed with their reasons.
- D5 Keyboard: Enter picks up, arrows move between shelves, Enter places; screen reader
  announces item, shelf and result. Touch drag works.
- D6 No timer. No leaderboard.
- D7 A Try again button; the LMS gets the latest attempt.
- D8 The client sees completion and score per learner.

A short line under the panel: *Compare this with module 00's one-line ask. Same agent.
The difference is eight sentences you did not have to think of; you only had to answer.*

## Screen 4 · What the record is for

> The builder will not remember this conversation. It will not even be the same agent. What
> it gets is the eight decisions, each with its reason, and it builds to them. That is why
> the output of a brief is a list, not a feeling that you have been understood.

Two cards:

- **Decisions bind the builder.** "No timer" on the record means no timer. A guess has
  nowhere to live.
- **Reasons let the builder choose well.** "Induction, not a game" tells the builder what
  kind of thing it is making when it hits a case nobody wrote down.

## Screen 5 · Check

**Q1.** The agent asks five questions. You have time to answer two. What do you do?

- (a) Answer two and let it guess the rest. *Guessing is what module 00 showed. The three
  gaps will be filled.*
- (b) **Say "your defaults, except…" for the ones you disagree with.** *Yes. The recommended
  answers are there so that this is a complete reply in one line.*
- (c) Ask it to stop asking and start building. *It will, and it will fill the gaps.*

**Q2.** Halfway through round two the agent recommends a leaderboard "to drive engagement".
What is the right move?

- (a) Accept it; the agent knows what works. *The agent knows what is common. The record is
  where you say what this course is.*
- (b) **Decide, and have the reason recorded with it.** *Yes. "No leaderboard: induction, not
  a game" is a decision a builder can hold to.*
- (c) Tell it never to recommend things. *The recommendations are the fast path. Decline the
  ones that are wrong.*

## Screen 6 · Takeaway

> **The agent asks, you decide, the decisions are written down.** The builder works from the
> record, not from your memory of a conversation.

The box gains its first wall: **the record**.

## Statements

Module launched; interaction with each of the eight choices; two answers; module completed.
