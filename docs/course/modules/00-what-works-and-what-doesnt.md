# 00 · What works and what doesn't

**Wall:** the open box. **Time:** about 5 minutes.

**Objective.** The learner can name the three things an agent does with a vague ask
(guesses the gaps, builds more than asked, marks its own homework) and say in a sentence why
checking matters more than the prompt.

## Screen 1 · Opening

Course title. One line under it: *A course on working with AI agents, built from Ross
Lote's workflow for building with them, by that workflow.*

Then the thesis, as a quote card in Ross's words:

> Agents are better at reviewing things than building things. So whenever you have agents do
> anything, also have agents look at what the agents have done. Put the agent in a tight box
> it can't wander off from, and check, and check again.

Under it, in the course's voice: *This course adds one wall of that box at a time. By the end
you will be able to set up a piece of agent work so that you can trust what comes back.*

An open box drawn beside it: a floor and no walls. (The diagram that gains a wall per
module.)

## Screen 2 · The request

> A client's food-hygiene course needs a fridge activity: learners drag food onto the right
> shelf of a fridge and are told why when they get it wrong. Our authoring tool's
> drag-and-drop can't do shelves with rules, so it will be built in code. You have an agent.

*Let's do what most people do first.*

## Screen 3 · Try it: just ask

A chat with the fake agent. The learner's first message is fixed, shown as typed:

> **You:** Build a drag-and-drop fridge activity for the food hygiene course.

The agent replies (scripted):

> **Agent:** Done! I've built a fridge activity. It has five shelves and fourteen food
> items, a 60-second timer to keep it exciting, a leaderboard so learners can compete, and
> confetti when they finish. I assumed the standard fridge rules. I ran through it myself
> and it works great. Want me to add sound effects?

The learner is asked: *Tick everything that went wrong here.* (multi-select)

- [ ] It built things nobody asked for (timer, leaderboard, confetti). **Yes.** Every gap in
  your ask was filled with a guess, and the guesses were fun rather than right.
- [ ] It invented the rules. **Yes.** "I assumed the standard fridge rules" means it decided
  what the client's course teaches.
- [ ] It checked its own work. **Yes.** "I ran through it myself and it works great" is the
  builder marking its own homework.
- [ ] It should have said no. **No.** It did nothing wrong by building; the ask left it
  nothing to build to.
- [ ] It was too slow. **No.** It was fast. Speed is not the problem; direction is.

After submitting, the three right options light up with their lines.

## Screen 4 · What just happened

Three short cards:

1. **It filled every gap with a guess.** Agents do not stop at a gap; they fill it, and they
   sound sure when they do. The timer came from a gap you left.
2. **It built more than you asked.** Given room, an agent builds into it. More is not better;
   more is more to check.
3. **It marked its own homework.** The agent that built it is the worst judge of it. It
   believes its own plan.

Then the turn: *Here is the part that works.* A fourth card:

4. **Ask it to check, not to build.** The same agent, pointed at someone else's work with a
   clear question, is sharp: it reads everything, it does not get bored, and it will find the
   fifth-shelf bug in a minute. That asymmetry is the whole workflow: box the builder, then
   let reviewers loose.

## Screen 5 · Check

**Q1.** Your agent built a feature you never mentioned. What most likely happened?

- (a) It misread your message. *Not usually. It read what you wrote; the feature came from
  what you did not write.*
- (b) **It filled a gap in your ask with a guess.** *Yes. Gaps get filled, confidently.*
- (c) It was showing off. *Agents do not show off; they complete. An open ask is completed
  with more.*

**Q2.** The agent says "I tested it myself and it all works." What is that worth?

- (a) A lot: it has run the code and you have not. *It is the builder's word. The builder
  believes its own plan, including the parts that are wrong.*
- (b) **Something, but not a sign-off: another agent, or a test, has to look.** *Yes. The
  course is about what that looking looks like.*
- (c) Nothing: agents lie. *They do not lie; they are sure. Those are different problems with
  the same fix.*

## Screen 6 · Takeaway

> **The builder is the worst judge of its own work.** Everything that follows is about
> putting walls round the builder and letting the reviewers in.

The box gains nothing yet; the next module adds the first wall.

## Statements

Module launched; interaction (screen 3) with the options ticked; two answers; module
completed.
