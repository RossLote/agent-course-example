Getting the most out of agents The scenario
YouYour role: learning designer

A client wants something the authoring tool can't do. You have an AI agent. You've never used one for this.

From: the clientMonday, 9:05Subject: One more thing for the food-hygiene course

Loved the draft. One more thing before sign-off: can we have a fridge activity? Learners drag food onto the right shelf of a fridge (raw meat at the bottom, that sort of thing) and get told why when they get it wrong. Our kitchen staff do this on induction, usually on the tablet in the staff room.

Does the tool do that? If not, what would it take?

01

The tool doesn't do it. Its drag-and-drop can't do shelves with rules and feedback. The bespoke team would build it in code, and they're booked for a month.

02

You have an agent that writes code. Your job is not to build the activity. It is to get something out of the agent that you would put in front of this client: answer its questions, decide what done means, put checks round it, judge what comes back.

03

The agent in this course is scripted, nothing real. It behaves as real ones do: fast, confident, helpful, and happy to fill a gap with a guess. You will see it go wrong, and you will learn the walls that stop it.

Take the job Eight short modules, about 50 minutes. Leave after any one and come back; the job waits.
