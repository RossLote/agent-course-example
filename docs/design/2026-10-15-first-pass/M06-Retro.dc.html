A true story

The check that missed

Not every miss is the client's taste. Sometimes a wall has a gap. This happened, in Ross's work, in October 2026.

1

A feature was built twice, on purpose: once by hand by a colleague, once by this workflow, with every agent told to ignore the other attempt. The question was whether the workflow could get to a passable result on its own.

2

A reviewer on the hand-built version asked for one change: a permission check looked at whether a field was present in the request, when it should have looked at whether the field was changing. An ordinary user who fetched a record, changed something else and sent it all back would be refused.

3

The workflow's version had the identical check. It had been written into the epic as a settled decision, so no review questioned it. The adversarial review found three other things and confirmed none. Two automated review rounds and two more review passes found nine things each. None of them was this.

4

The workflow had a gap: nothing in it asked what an ordinary caller would do. The decision was settled, so every agent respected it.

5

What changed, in the days after. A new lens for the finders: what would an ordinary caller do? The skeptic's rule tightened: "the code does what the spec says" is not a refutation when the spec is the problem. The rule that a refutation must cite a line. And a new step, the retro, that reads several runs' records back to find what goes wrong more than once.

The agent was not told to be more careful. The check was changed.
